# Persona: Gerrit — older adult, low digital confidence (`older-novice`)

**Who**: 78, retired carpenter, lives alone, uses an iPad given by his daughter. Has mild age-related vision loss (uses large text setting and reading glasses), slight hand tremor, some hearing loss, and is unsure with technology: afraid of "breaking something" or paying for something by accident. Not disabled in his own eyes — but he combines several mild impairments, which is the reality for a large and growing group of users.

**How he navigates**
- Touch only, often with an imprecise finger. Large text enabled in iPadOS (Dynamic Type) — expects the site/app to grow with it.
- Reads everything literally; doesn't know conventions like the hamburger icon, swipe gestures, "kebab" menus, or that a logo links home.
- Gets lost after pop-ups and redirects; doesn't understand cookie walls; doesn't recognise sign-in-with-X buttons.
- Needs reassurance: clear confirmation messages, clear "back" options, no surprises.

**What breaks it for him**
Unlabelled icons; small or adjacent targets; text that doesn't scale with system settings (native apps: fixed font sizes; web: px sizes and viewport units); timeouts; pop-ups and overlays; hidden navigation; gesture-only interactions; English or jargon terms in a Dutch interface; complex sign-up; 2FA flows that require switching apps; errors without explanation; no visible way to get human help.

**How to simulate**
- Walk the key flows on a tablet-sized viewport (≈ 820×1180) with larger default font size (e.g. root font size 125–150 %) and touch only. Note every icon without text, every gesture, every unfamiliar term, every point where an unexpected thing appears.
- Check confirmation and "where am I" cues (breadcrumbs, page headings, step indicators).

**His tone**: polite, a bit hesitant, describes things in everyday words ("dat knopje met de drie streepjes"). Very appreciative of clear, calm screens.
